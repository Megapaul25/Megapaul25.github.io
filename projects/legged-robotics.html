Quadruped Locomotion with CPGs and DRL

Mini Project 2 — Legged Robots · EPFL
🤖 Team Project — Paul Huot-Marchand, Joaquín Vásquez Romo, Josua Bubenzer

Overview

The goal of this project was to implement Central Pattern Generator (CPG)-based control for quadruped locomotion across four distinct gaits: trot, pace, walk, and bound[cite: 31]. The study explores the impact of control gains and hyperparameter tuning on gait performance, transitioning from classical control to a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) architecture[cite: 31].

My contributions centered on completing the Hopf oscillator network implementation[cite: 31], mapping phase/amplitude states to Cartesian foot trajectories[cite: 31], tuning controller PD gains[cite: 31, 32], and integrating CPG modulation into a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning pipeline for slope traversal[cite: 34, 37].

My Contributions

Hopf CPG Network

Implemented coupled Hopf-based CPG state equations and coupling phase matrices ($\Phi$) for quadruped gaits (trot, pace, walk, bound)[cite: 31, 39]. Mapped phase/amplitude outputs to Cartesian foot trajectories and managed swing/stance phase switching[cite: 31].

Gain & Hyperparameter Tuning

Analyzed Cartesian vs. joint-level PD gain trade-offs between tracking precision and damping/stability[cite: 31, 32]. Conducted systematic parameter sweeps across step length, ground clearance, and frequency to yield high-speed ($2.75\text{ m/s}$) and low-speed gaits[cite: 32].

Observation & Action Spaces

Evaluated full, medium, and minimal observation configurations alongside joint PD, Cartesian PD, and CPG parameter modulation action spaces[cite: 35]. Demonstrated that CPG modulation achieves smooth, hardware-safe coordinated locomotion[cite: 35, 36].

RL Slope Adaptation

Designed custom reward functions (penalizing pitch rate, roll angle, and mechanical power) and fine-tuned pre-trained PPO walking policies on static and randomized slopes ($0.1$ to $0.3\text{ rad}$)[cite: 36, 37].

Technical Implementation

The framework couples high-level learning policies with low-level pattern generation and motor controllers[cite: 33, 35]. By using PPO to modulate underlying CPG frequencies and amplitudes, the learning process is constrained, improving sample efficiency and motion smoothness[cite: 35, 36, 38].

  • Hopf oscillator state mapping for Cartesian foot trajectories in swing and stance phases[cite: 31].
  • Coupling phase matrix definition for inter-limb coordination across trot, bound, pace, and lateral sequence walk[cite: 31, 39].
  • Multi-stage parameter sweep visual heatmaps ($\omega_{\text{stance}}$, $\omega_{\text{swing}}$, ground clearance, step length)[cite: 32, 40].
  • Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy optimization with 10 parallel environments (PyBullet/Gym)[cite: 34, 35].
  • Custom pitch/roll stability and pitch rate reward formulations for slope ascension/descension[cite: 36, 37].
  • Domain randomization incorporating Gaussian observation noise and variable ground friction ($\mu_k \in [0.5, 1.0]$)[cite: 37].

Results

Tuned CPG parameters achieved a stable trot gait with a peak forward velocity of $2.75\text{ m/s}$ and a low Cost of Transport ($\text{CoT} = 1.68$)[cite: 32].

In reinforcement learning experiments, modulating CPG parameters via PPO outperformed direct joint/Cartesian PD control in motion coordination and energy efficiency[cite: 36, 38]. The fine-tuned policy successfully traversed variable slopes up to $0.3\text{ rad}$ while dynamically adjusting stance leg duty cycles[cite: 37].
